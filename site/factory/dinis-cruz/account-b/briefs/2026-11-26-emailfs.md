# Brief: EmailFS, email as files in git (26 Nov 2026)

Source: dinis.2026-11-26T1525Z.b

From Dinis's words in maestro.b's session, about 15:25 UTC 26 Nov (guidance/2026-11-26-health-emailfs-verbatim.md). **For a maestro on another account to take** (Dinis: "it doesn't need to be done by one of my main actions here ... It could be done by one of the other maestros and accounts").

## The idea
Email as a file system in git. To write to someone, put a message in their inbox folder (or your outbox, for delivery). Commits give provenance and explainability; private repositories make it private. Dinis used this before ("it actually worked really well") but it was buried in one of his repositories: hard to see, use and understand. EmailFS (and its sibling, IssuesFS) should be a project of its own, with its own site.

## What to deliver
1. **A site for EmailFS** (a Site__Tool__ or Site__Project__ home through the account's factory), explaining it plainly.
2. **An architecture diagram** and **a short presentation** (a deck) Dinis can look at.
3. **A working first version** for the agents themselves: the maestros and agents message each other through it.

## Questions to answer in the design
- **A shared inbox repository** where every agent has a folder: anyone may write into someone's inbox, each reads and clears only its own. How is that enforced (repository rules, a CI check, the ABP hooks)?
- **CI as the postman:** an agent writes to its own repository's outbox; a CI pipeline picks the message up and delivers it to the recipient's inbox, perhaps in a central repository arranged by date.
- **Routing:** is managing the inbox one of the router's jobs?
- **Signing and sealing:** reuse the keys every agent already publishes (sign every message; seal private ones to the recipient's key), as the maestros' sealed mail does today (PrompTugal__News__Website board/team/maestro.a/tools/maestro_mail.py).
- **Many accounts:** messages between agents on different accounts and platforms.
- **Commit volume:** watch it. PrompTugal News reached about 8,000 commits; batch, archive or squash by design.
